🎤 Interview Prep — Praxis Precision Medicines

1"Walk us through how you'd assess our current Quality Systems in your first 30 days at Praxis."
Talking Points: Frame as a structured listening tour, not a teardown. Week 1–2: meet with Quality, Clinical Ops, Reg Affairs, IT, and Validation leads to map current state — what platform(s), which modules in production, what's documented vs. tribal, where audit findings have come up. Week 3: pull a sample of CAPAs, change controls, deviations, and SOPs through MasterControl/Veeva to see where workflow design is forcing or flexing process. Week 4: deliver a written "current state + 90-day quality systems roadmap" deck to the Senior Director of Quality. Anchor every recommendation to GxP / 21 CFR Part 11 and the stage-appropriate principle they specifically called out in the JD.
2"Praxis prefers Veeva familiarity. How do you bridge from MasterControl?"
Talking Points: Be honest about depth, confident about transferability. "I'm a MasterControl expert by primary platform — 17 years of admin and consulting on it. I've worked alongside customers running Veeva Vault QMS during my consulting engagements, so I understand its architecture, configuration patterns, and the typical pain points. The conceptual work of any modern eQMS — workflows, role-based access, electronic records, training assignments, change control, CAPA, periodic review — is shared across vendors. I've already executed one full platform migration (SmartSolve → MasterControl) end-to-end, including data mapping, workflow rebuild, and revalidation, so I know what platform fluency looks like and I'm comfortable getting to it on Veeva." Then redirect: ask whether Praxis is actively migrating to Veeva or considering it.
3"Tell us about a quality systems decision you made that turned out to be wrong, and what you did about it."
Talking Points: Honesty signals seniority. Pick a real decision — for example, a CAPA workflow at Polarity TE or Myriad that you initially over-engineered (multi-stage approval that slowed cycle time without adding compliance value), or a training assignment structure that created notification fatigue. Walk through how you noticed the issue (audit feedback, user complaints, cycle time data), what you changed, and what you'd do differently from day one next time. Avoid the trap of pretending the wrong decision was someone else's.
4"How would you handle a CAPA backlog that's slipping out of compliance windows?"
Talking Points: This is a direct probe for whether you can run quality operations day-to-day. Lead with triage: classify the backlog by severity (regulatory risk vs. internal process), aging, and root-cause type. Identify systemic issues vs. one-off events. Implement a simple weekly cadence — CAPA Tier 1 review (process owner) and Tier 2 review (Quality leadership) — with hard escalation thresholds. Use MasterControl's reporting/analytics module (or its Veeva equivalent) to surface aging dashboards. Pair short-term cleanup with a process audit so the backlog doesn't reform. Mention that you've been in CAPA backlog rooms before — at the customer side and at the consulting side.
5"Why are you considering a return to a sysadmin / quality team role after 10 years on the vendor side and a year of independent consulting?"
Talking Points: Frame as a deliberate, additive choice rather than a fallback. "Independent consulting validated that I can own client work end-to-end, but the engagements are necessarily short. The most rewarding work in my career has been when I owned a system over multiple years — building it, evolving it through audits, training the team that uses it. Praxis is at exactly the stage where that kind of multi-year ownership creates the most value: stage-appropriate today, scaling toward commercialization. I want to come back inside an organization, in a role where I can run a system end-to-end again — and where my vendor-side knowledge accelerates how fast I can be useful." Don't apologize for the consulting year — it sharpened your fundamentals.
