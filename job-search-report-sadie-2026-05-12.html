Interview Prep — Salt Lake County DA

5 Questions You Should Expect

1. "Walk me through a time you handled a high-stress situation involving an upset person."
  • Use a server/bartender de-escalation story — be specific.
  • Frame: stay calm, listen first, separate facts from emotion, take action.
  • Land it with: "That's the same posture I'd take with a victim sitting in the witness room — calm, present, and focused on what they need to feel safe."
2. "What does trauma-informed care mean to you?"
  • Define it in plain language: safety, choice, transparency, empowerment, cultural humility.
  • Cite where you learned it (Sociology of Human Behavior, independent reading).
  • Concrete example: don't ask "what happened" before asking "are you safe right now."
3. "Why this office? Why not a nonprofit?"
  • Answer: "Both matter — but I want to learn the prosecutorial pipeline because that's the system survivors are navigating, and where I want to spend my legal career."
  • Reference a Salt Lake County DA initiative if you've researched one (e.g., diversion programs, Drug Court, the office's stance on victim notification).
4. "How do you maintain confidentiality and emotional boundaries?"
  • Confidentiality: cite Canopy Mortgage compliance experience — you've literally done this on a job.
  • Emotional boundaries: name your support system (mentors, classmates), your routines (exercise, golf, journaling), and your willingness to use the office's EAP/supervision structure.
5. "Where do you want to be in 5 years?"
  • Be honest: in law school or finishing it, on a track toward prosecution or victim rights work.
  • Address the "she'll leave for law school" concern head-on: "I'm seeking a 1–2 year experience here precisely because it's the right foundation for the lawyer I want to be — and grant-funded roles align with that horizon."

3 Questions You Should Ask Them

  • "How does the Victim Services unit coordinate with the deputy DAs on case-by-case basis — what does a typical hand-off look like?"
  • "What's the most common training your team wishes new coordinators arrived with? I'm scheduling the UCASA 40-hour this summer."
  • "Beyond the day-to-day, where does your team feel you most need additional capacity right now?"

Quick Company Brief

  • Salt Lake County is Utah's largest county; the DA's office handles felony prosecution for the entire county.
  • The Victim Services unit sits alongside the prosecution teams and follows survivors from investigation through sentencing and restitution.
  • The office runs specialty courts (Mental Health, Drug, Domestic Violence) that victim coordinators support.
  • The DA is an elected position — research the current DA's published priorities before the interview.
  • Many alumni of victim services roles move into law enforcement, paralegal work, or law school within 1–3 years — your timeline is normal here, not a red flag.
