Five likely questions, with talking points

1. Walk me through how you manage a CEO's calendar in a week where everything is competing for time.
Anchor the answer in your Gen4 routine. Talk about a Sunday/Monday review with the CEO to set "protected" blocks (board prep, deep work, family), color-coding by priority, building a 24-hour rolling re-prioritization habit, and the "decline / delegate / defer / do" filter you apply to incoming requests.
2. Tell me about a time you handled highly confidential information.
Use Barclays as the lead example — MNPI handling on a deal team — then bridge to Gen4 (HIPAA-adjacent communications, M&A activity for a DSO). The point is: you've operated inside two of the most controlled information environments that exist.
3. How do you support multiple principals — for instance, a CEO plus a Chief Experience Officer and a Chief Health Officer — without one feeling deprioritized?
Reference Magnit, where you supported a Chief Client Officer alongside a partner exec. Talk about having an explicit "primary," shared calendars with the secondary principals, weekly 15-minute syncs with each, and a single shared "asks queue" so nothing slips between cracks.
4. How do you handle a CEO who's traveling internationally on a tight schedule?
Walk through your travel runbook: ground transport pre-booked door-to-door, contingency flight options held, time-zone-shifted prep packets sent the night before, an in-region escalation contact, and proactive expense reconciliation so nothing piles up. Reference Barclays international travel for credibility.
5. What does "trusted partner to a CEO" mean to you?
Two-part answer. First: the CEO trusts you with their time, their inbox, and their reputation — so you treat each like an asset. Second: you push back when something is a bad use of their day, and you do it in private. The relationship works because you've earned the right to disagree.
